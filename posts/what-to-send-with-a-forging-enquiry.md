A forging quote depends on a handful of facts. When they arrive together, the forge can price the die, the steel and the machining in one go. When they arrive one at a time, the quote takes days. Here is what to send.

## The essentials

- **A drawing.** A PDF or DWG with dimensions and tolerances is best. If you have no drawing, a clear photo of a sample with its main sizes, or the sample itself, is the next best thing.
- **Material grade.** Carbon, alloy or stainless steel, and the grade if you know it. If the part replaces an existing one, say what the original was made of.
- **Quantity.** A trial lot, a one-off batch or a monthly schedule. Forging dies pay back over volume, so this changes the price per piece the most.
- **What it goes into.** "Scraper link for a cement conveyor" or "rod end for a loader cylinder" tells the forge how the part is loaded.

## Details that save a second round

- **Finish.** Which faces, bores and threads must be machined, and to what tolerance.
- **Hardness or heat treatment requirement**, if your drawing specifies one.
- **Inspection.** Any test you need on the parts, for example magnetic particle inspection or a hardness report.
- **Packing and delivery.** Where the parts go and how they should be packed.

## Buying from outside India

If you are sourcing from overseas, add the delivery terms you prefer and any marking or documentation your customers need. Send the same drawing and grade details as above; that is what the quote is built on.
